Scranly Privacy Policy

Last updated 18 November 2026 · applies to Scranly 1.0 for iPhone

Scranly has no account and no login. What you eat, what you weigh and what you told it about yourself are written to your phone and stay there. Two things leave the phone, both described below, and neither carries your name.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored in Scranly's own storage on the device:

  • The details you gave on the About you screen, and the daily budget worked out from them.
  • Every food you log, and the day you logged it against.
  • Every weight you record.

This is not encrypted beyond the protection iOS already gives an app's own files, it is not backed up to any server of mine, and I cannot read it. Deleting the app deletes it.

What leaves your phone

Two requests, both of which you cause by doing something.

Looking up a food — Open Food Facts

When you scan a barcode or search, Scranly sends the barcode you scanned or the words you typed, plus a line naming the app and my email address, which is how their API asks developers to identify themselves.

It does not send your diary, your profile, your weight, or anything identifying you.

Open Food Facts is an open food database run by a French non-profit. Like any website, it can see the IP address a request comes from. Their own privacy terms are at openfoodfacts.org/privacy.

Sending feedback — only when you tap send

The feedback screen sends which of the suggestions you tapped, the two things you wrote, and which version of the app you were using.

It does not send your name, your email, your diary, or any device identifier. I cannot tell who sent a piece of feedback unless you write your name in the box.

It goes through Web3Forms, a service that receives the message and emails it to me. It reaches ezekielayikoe9@gmail.com and nowhere else.

The camera

Scranly asks for the camera so it can read a barcode. The reading happens on the phone. No photo or video frame is saved, and none is sent anywhere — only the digits of the barcode are, and only to look up the food.

If you refuse camera access the app still works: search for a food instead, or type one in.

What Scranly does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no profile on any server.
  • No analytics, no usage tracking, no advertising, no advertising identifier.
  • No location, no contacts, no photo library, no Apple Health data.
  • No third-party tracking libraries of any kind. There are none in the app.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone, because there is nothing held to sell or share.

While Scranly is in TestFlight

Scranly is currently distributed as a test build through Apple's TestFlight. Apple collects crash reports and basic usage information from TestFlight builds under its own terms, and shows me crash logs and the number of sessions. That collection is Apple's, not mine, and is covered by Apple's privacy policy and the terms you accepted in TestFlight.

Children

Scranly is not designed for or directed at children under 13, and nothing in it asks for an age.

Your control over the data

Because the data is on your device and nowhere else, you hold all of it. Deleting the app removes it. There is no account to close and no request to send me, because I am not holding a copy.

Feedback you have already sent arrives as an email and sits in my inbox. If you want a message withdrawn, write to me and say roughly when you sent it and I will delete it.

Changes

If Scranly starts doing something with data that this page does not describe, this page changes first and the date at the top changes with it.
